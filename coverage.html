Editorial scope

What we cover

A site this size works better when it says no to things. Here is what we write about, what we deliberately skip, and how we decide.

Why publish this

Almost every gaming site covers everything, which helps nobody. Somebody who wants to know whether to buy a console does not want a mobile game round-up, and somebody deep in a competitive shooter community does not want a console buying guide.

So we state our scope plainly. If we have told you we cover something and then do not cover it, that is a mistake worth reporting.

What we cover regularly

PlayStation hardware and setup

  • Console setup, storage expansion and the settings that matter
  • Accessories, and whether they earn their price
  • What a console upgrade does and does not solve
  • News that affects players: firmware, store changes, service changes

PC gaming decisions

  • Graphics card choices at 1080p, 1440p and 4K
  • Which PC upgrades genuinely pay for themselves
  • Settings that fix common problems: frame pacing, input latency, upscaling
  • Port versus console build quality

Comparisons between the platforms

  • Total cost of ownership, peripherals included
  • Game libraries, exclusivity windows and the cost of owning games twice
  • Cross-play and cross-save
  • Controllers versus mouse and keyboard

How people play

  • Accessibility settings on both platforms
  • Playing in a shared living room
  • What a platform means for a household with mixed devices

What we deliberately do not cover

  • Rumour and speculation. Unverified leaks, unless we are reporting them as unverified leaks and naming the source.
  • Specific prices. Console and component prices change too fast and too regionally for us to quote figures we cannot keep current. We will tell you what to look at and when we checked, but we will not publish a price we know will be stale.
  • Single benchmark numbers without context. A frame rate without the game, the settings and the display is not information.
  • Cheap accessory round-ups. We will tell you a storage expansion is worth it. We will not list ten unbranded pads.
  • Sponsored conclusions. Never, at any price. Our approach is set out on the About page.
  • Games we have not spent time with. We do not write impressions from trailers or marketing material.

How we decide what to write

  1. Somebody is deciding something. Buying a console, choosing a graphics card, working out why a game looks wrong. Advice beats news.
  2. The answer is not obvious. If the honest answer is "it depends, and here is how to decide", that is a better article than a verdict nobody needs.
  3. It will still be true in six months. We would rather publish one guide that stays useful than ten pieces chasing a week's news.
  4. We can say where the information came from. Tested ourselves, published by a vendor, or reported by someone else and attributed.

Corrections and gaps

If we get something wrong we fix it. Small errors go unannounced. Anything that changes a conclusion gets a dated note on the page.

If you have noticed a topic we should be covering, we want to hear it. Several of our guides exist because a reader asked.
