PS5 or PC? A straight answer

Both platforms are good. The difference is not quality, it is what kind of gaming you want. This guide works through cost, games, performance and longevity so you can work out which one is built for you.

Updated 12 Jan 2026 No sponsored verdicts Prices dated where quoted

The one-paragraph version. Buy a PlayStation 5 if you want games to just work on the television, if the exclusives matter to you, or if nobody in your house enjoys opening a PC case. Buy a PC if you want the best settings at the highest frame rates, if you already play a lot of the games that are on both, or if you want your spending to stretch further over five years. If you play competitive shooters at high ranks, lean PC. If you play single-player story games and want zero friction, lean PlayStation.

Work out which one you are

Spec sheets do not decide this question. Your habits do. Answer these five honestly and the answer usually becomes obvious.

1. Do you care about console exclusives?

If there is a specific game you want that never comes to PC, or comes much later, that is a genuine reason to buy PlayStation and it outweighs almost everything else on this page. If no exclusive matters to you, this question is already answered in favour of PC.

2. How much of your gaming is competitive?

High-rank shooters and competitive multiplayer are the area where mouse and keyboard, high refresh rates and low input latency combine. None of that is available on a console, and console versions of the same games often restrict your settings. If this describes you, PC is the better tool. If you mostly play single-player games, it matters much less.

3. Who else uses the television?

A console is a shared household appliance. It is fast, quiet and hard to break by accident. A gaming PC in the living room needs a good keyboard, a controller, and a display that makes desktop windows usable. If the PC will live in a study and you will game at a desk, that is not a problem at all.

4. Will you enjoy maintaining it?

Be honest here. A PC that needs a new graphics card in four years, or a Windows install that needs rebuilding, is only a bargain for someone who is comfortable with it. If you would rather never think about it, buy the console.

5. Are you already buying the peripheral kit?

Compare like with like. The console price does not include a second controller, extra storage or a headset. A PC price usually does not include a monitor, keyboard or mouse. Whichever option looks cheaper on a website is often not cheaper once the accessories are counted.

Buying a PC instead? Do not skip this step

The most common mistake is not choosing wrong between platforms, it is buying PC parts that do not match each other. A processor too weak for the graphics card, or memory on the slow side, wastes money you cannot get back.

  1. Decide your resolution first. It sets your graphics card budget more than anything else. 1080p, 1440p and 4K are different purchases.
  2. Match the graphics card to that resolution. Buying a high-end card and playing at 1080p leaves most of it unused.
  3. Do not over-invest in the processor. For gaming, the graphics card matters far more than extra CPU cores.
  4. Get adequate memory. 16 GB is a sensible floor in 2026 for comfortable multi-tasking alongside games.
  5. Do not forget the peripherals. Monitor, keyboard, mouse, headset and any controller you need are all extra, and often substantial.
  6. Check your room and power. Confirm physical space, ventilation and that your power and network setup will handle it.
