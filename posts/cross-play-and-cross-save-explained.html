Cross-play and cross-save, explained simply

Two features that solve two different problems, routinely confused with each other. Once you can tell them apart, choosing a platform gets much easier.

Published 22 December 2025 · Updated 22 December 2025 · 5 min read · by PC Games vs PlayStation

If your friend plays on a PlayStation and you are thinking about building a PC, or the other way around, these are the two features that decide whether you can actually play together and whether you lose your progress when you switch.

The difference in one line each

  • Cross-play lets players on different platforms play the same multiplayer game together, in the same match.
  • Cross-save lets your saved game data follow you between platforms, so you can continue where you stopped.

They are independent. A game can have one and not the other. You can play with your friends on a different platform but still have to start your campaign again on your own console. That combination is common and it is the source of most confusion.

Cross-play: playing together

Cross-play is now close to standard in the major competitive shooters. If you own a PS5 and a friend owns a PC, you can usually queue into the same matchmaking pool.

What to check before you rely on it:

  • Does this specific game support it? It is not universal. Some titles remain separated by platform entirely, and a few support cross-play in some modes but not others.
  • Which platform's version are you on? Cross-play normally works best when both players are on the newest version of the game. If one of you is on a last-generation console, the game may put you in a separate matchmaking pool.
  • Is there a cross-play setting to enable? Some games on some platforms let you turn cross-play off. If you cannot see your friends in the list, check that it is on.
  • Can your platform be excluded entirely? In a few titles, console cross-play can be disabled, usually for competitive reasons. Where that is the case, PC players get the wider pool and console players get a narrower one.

On PlayStation, linking your account to Discord and enabling friends who own the game in Discord settings is worth doing. It lets you see which of your friends are online in a game you own, and join their party, without hunting through the console's own interface.

Cross-save: keeping your progress

Cross-save matters when you own more than one platform, or when you switch. It means your unlocked weapons, campaign progress, inventory and in-game currency carry across.

How it works in practice:

  1. Sign in to the same account on both platforms. On PlayStation, that means the same PlayStation Network account. On PC, it usually means linking the game's own account or a platform account.
  2. Check the game specifically. Cross-save has to be supported by the game, and support is not universal. There is no setting on the console that forces a game to support it.
  3. Progress uploads on save. When you finish on one platform, your data is uploaded and the other platform picks it up, usually the next time you load the game there.

Two behaviours surprise people. First, some games take a while to sync, so a switch between platforms immediately after saving can occasionally show stale data. Second, a small number of games restrict cross-save to specific content, letting you carry a campaign forward but not competitive progression, or the reverse.

PS5, PS4 and between generations

There is a separate, older feature worth knowing about, and it is more reliable than cross-save.

Within the PlayStation ecosystem, many PS4 games support being played on a PS5 with your PS4 progress carried over, and many PS4 games are available on PS5 through the PlayStation Store. This is native platform support rather than a per-game cross-save scheme, so it tends to work more reliably and more widely.

The practical version of this: if you owned a PS4 library, a PS5 is unusually good value even setting new games aside. Check your PS4 library on the PS5 before deciding that a console buys you nothing you already have.

Two traps worth knowing about

Cross-save is not the same as cross-progression, and neither is guaranteed. Where a game supports carrying progress across, some cosmetic items, event rewards or competitive statistics may be tied to one platform or one account. This varies by game.

Buying the console version does not give you the PC version. A digital PlayStation purchase is a PlayStation purchase. If a game later arrives on PC, it is a separate purchase at a separate price. This is the single most common misunderstanding in the platforms debate, and it is a real ongoing cost. Our guide comparing the two libraries covers the practical cost of owning games twice.

What this means for choosing a platform

Cross-play has done a great deal to remove the strongest historical objection to buying a PlayStation: it was once a real risk that choosing one meant losing access to your friends. That risk is now mostly about older console generations and the occasional game without support.

Cross-save has done the same for switching between your own machines, though it still depends on the game.

So the decision is now mostly about hardware, settings and cost rather than about being locked out of your friends. That is a genuinely different position from a few years ago, and it is worth remembering when you read older comparisons. Our buying guide works through what is left.

A note on patch versions. Multiplayer games on the same platform will usually not matchmake with players on a different patch, which happens when one version is paused or rolled back. If you cannot find your friends, check that both of you are on the current version. This is not cross-play failing; it is a version mismatch.
